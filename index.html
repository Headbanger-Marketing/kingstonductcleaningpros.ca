Answering a reminder that says the ducts are due for cleaning

The details of the home and any earlier job record belong in every Kingston request. A reminder, though, is written from a date in a provider's records rather than from your home today. If you received one, it may not say what was cleaned last time or what the provider noted. List the earlier invoice details, any findings recorded, and what you can see now at registers, return grilles and the dryer hood. Those details let the responding provider quote against the system's present condition rather than the reminder.

  1. Return grilles

    Openings that pull room air back into the system.

  2. Return ducts

    The runs leading from each grille to the furnace.

  3. Furnace blower and coil

    The blower, evaporator coil and cabinet.

  4. Supply trunk

    The main duct leaving the furnace.

  5. Branch runs

    The individual runs that split off toward each room.

  6. Supply registers

    The floor, wall or ceiling vents in each room.

Separate runs to ask about:Dryer ventHRV core and screensBathroom fan ductsRange hood exhaust

  • The earlier job record

    Gather any previous invoice or report showing which parts of the system were cleaned and any findings the provider wrote down.

  • What you see today

    Note dust at registers, staining, odours or weak airflow in named rooms, or write that you have noticed nothing unusual.

  • The reminder itself

    If a reminder arrived, keep the card, email or call details so the request can show who sent it and what it claimed about the work.

Questions About Your Kingston Request

Who would clean my ducts, and did Kingston Duct Cleaning Pros send me the reminder?

Kingston Duct Cleaning Pros is an enquiry website, not a contractor, and it did not send the reminder you hold. It has no technicians, vehicles or premises of its own. Once our enquiry team has reviewed an inquiry, it may go to a responding provider. That provider must identify themselves and confirm they can serve your address before anything is arranged.

What happens in Kingston after I send my reminder and earlier record?

What you sent is read first by our enquiry team. A responding provider may then reply with questions or a proposed scope. After any reply, you decide whether to go ahead, and you can decline at any point before agreeing in writing. On its own, sending the request arranges nothing, sets no price and does not confirm that a provider covers your address. Keep a copy of what you sent.

Does a reminder from a previous provider mean my ducts need cleaning?

Not by itself. A reminder reflects the sender's records and not an inspection of your system today. Compare it with the earlier job record and with what you can observe at the registers, the dryer hood and any ventilator. If you see nothing unusual, say so in the request and ask the provider to explain what new work would add. Keep the reminder with your request.

How do I check a reminder card before agreeing to cleaning in my Kingston home?

Look for the sender's name, the date of the earlier job and what the card says was done. Match those against your invoice. If the details do not line up, or the sender cannot be identified, send the card itself with your request so a responding provider can see exactly what was claimed before agreeing to anything.

Can I ask about the dryer vent and the ducts in one Kingston request?

Yes, but describe each system separately. A dryer exhaust is its own run, and an earlier duct record may not mention it. State whether the reminder named the vent, send photographs of the outside hood, and ask the responding provider to list each system's scope, with any added item on its own line. Mention any lint you have noticed.

What if I have no earlier record of the last cleaning?

Say so in the request. Describe what you remember, such as which rooms, whether the dryer vent or a ventilator was included, and who did the work. A responding provider can still assess the system as it is now, but should explain what could not be compared and note it in the written scope. Photographs of the present system help fill the gap.

Who can approve the work if the reminder reached me as a tenant?

Approval may rest with the landlord, property manager or condominium corporation, rather than the person who received the notice. Name who owns the system, who pays and who can give access. A provider should not proceed until the person with authority has agreed to the written scope and the arrangements for access. Name the contact for each.

What should I photograph when a reminder mentions a treatment or repair?

Photograph any stained or damp-looking area, the registers nearby, visible joints and access panels, and the earlier invoice lines naming a product or repair. Describe the room and when you noticed it. A treatment or repair should be quoted as its own item, separate from cleaning, so you can compare each with what the reminder claimed.
